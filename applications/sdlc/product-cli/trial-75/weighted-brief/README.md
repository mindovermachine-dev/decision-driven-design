# Fourth run: chapters weighted by what the reader needs

Letting the reader's needs decide how much space each chapter gets raised the brief from 5.5 to 7.5 of its reader's ten questions, in the same space and with nothing invented.

## What changed

Up to this run every chapter of the canon got one line in the brief. Now a model shares out 240 words among the chapters, by what the reader's description says they need. 240 words is the space the chapter lines had before.

A chapter given 50 words or more is written from the canon's own text. A chapter given less is written from its shared summary. A chapter given nothing is left out.

| Chapter | Words | Written from |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The promise | 75 | the canon |
| 2. Who it is for | 25 | its summary |
| 3. The surface | 40 | its summaries |
| 4. The formats | 0 | left out |
| 5. The guarantees | 60 | the canon |
| 6. The contracts between parts | 0 | left out |
| 7. Implementation | 0 | left out |
| 8. Boundaries and decisions taken | 40 | its summary |
| Cross-repository dependencies | 0 | left out |

## Results against predictions

The predictions were recorded on issue #75 before the run.

| Prediction | Result |
|---|---|
| 1. The new brief scores at least two questions higher than the previous one, in the same judging. | **Held.** 7.5 against 5.5. |
| 2. The check finds at most one sentence the cited sections do not support. | **Held.** 16 sentences and 9 explanations, all supported. |
| 3. Chapters 1 and 5 together get more than half of the 240 words. | **Held.** 135. |

## What else the run shows

- **The gain is where it was expected.** The previous brief missed three questions that rest on chapter 1. The new one answers two of them in full.
- **Two questions are still unanswered.** One asks which part of the definition can change without the product changing; the weighting gave the implementation chapter no words. The other asks what happens when a specification with a failing scenario is exported; that detail did not make the 60 words of chapter 5.
- **The brief is as useful as the earlier two-chapter projection at full depth,** which scored 7.5 in the same judging and was several times longer.
- **More terms are met, and more of them have no explanation.** The brief uses 14 terms. Five are shown as not explained: Writ, decision record, grant, change set and finding.

## Limits

- The ten questions were written from chapters 1 and 5. The weighting favoured those chapters, so the test favours this brief. The gain shows that weighting moves space to where these questions look. It does not show that these were the right questions.
- One reader, one run of each step, one judge. Earlier runs showed the judging moves by about one and a half questions between runs; this comparison is within one judging.
- The new brief is 320 words before its list of terms, against 295 before.
