# projections/tracks/

**Learning tracks — a single ordered path through part of the vocabulary, taught against one
worked decision that does not change.** A track is a projection like any other: filtered,
re-registered, pinned, manifested.

Filed 2026-08-18 under `DDD-dec-27` and ratified by Emil's acceptance; the `[PROPOSED]` banner
that stood here was struck 2026-10-26 (`DDD-dec-40`).

## What a track is

| | |
|---|---|
| **Filter** | one region of the graph, taken in one order |
| **Register** | engineering — the same node the formal register states as a theorem is stated here as something you do on Tuesday |
| **Status floor** | none, but every projected or draft node is **flagged at the point of use**, loudly |
| **Unit** | the **rung** — one idea, one move in the worked decision, one thing you could not do before |
| **Worked decision** | held constant while the vocabulary changes — that constancy is what lets a learner attribute the difficulty to the words rather than the example |

**One exception to constancy, and it must be argued rather than taken.** A terminal rung that asks
the learner to own an act needs something contested to be about: a discharge nobody can disagree
with makes naming a principal free. Where a track's running example was chosen for being
uncontested — which is right while the vocabulary is new — the terminal rung may introduce a
second decision that is not. The switch is stated to the learner at the point it happens, with the
reason, and recorded as an amendment to the track's own charter (`DDD-dec-27` §9 is the instance).
A substitution made silently, or made because an example merely strained, is out of order.

Tracks are audience-general in filing. **Audience is a manifest field, not a path segment.** A
track's content is Layer 1 and actor-general; only its *example* is domain-specific. Filing by
example domain would put a governance implementer's track and a canon contributor's track in
different trees for the same register, which is the wrong shape.

## The status floor rule, stated once

Practice-facing projections filter to established and reported, **or flag projected claims
loudly** (way-of-working §3). A track is as practice-facing as a projection gets — someone will
act on it — so the second half of that rule is doing the work, and it is not satisfied by a
manifest alone. **A reader who arrives mid-document must see a node's status without leaving the
document.** Flag at the point of use.

The same rule governs material that is not canon at all. A track may carry expository
scaffolding — a taxonomy, a mnemonic, a table that helps and claims nothing. Such material is
marked **track-native, not canon** in line, every time it appears, with the reason it is not
canon and what would change that.

## What a track is not

- **Not a competence claim.** Rungs supply **capability** — the class of ground you can now read
  and the distinctions you can now resolve against it (`term:capability`). Capability is the
  entry fee, not the qualification. A track that ends without the learner performing a real
  governed act has taught vocabulary and nothing else.
- **Not a derivation.** The rung order is a **declared decision with an owner**, not a
  consequence of the canon dependency order. It must be *consistent* with that order — no rung
  may need a term a later rung introduces — but consistency is a constraint the order satisfies,
  never the thing that produced it. Deriving the order from the dependency graph and calling it
  settled would be presumed discharge: the artefact recording the skip identical to the artefact
  recording the choice.
- **Not a reference.** Each rung moves the same worked decision one step. A reader who wants the
  claim reads the claim; a track exists to make the claim *reachable*.
